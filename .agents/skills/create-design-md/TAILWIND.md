# Tailwind colors

The Tailwind CSS 4.3 palette (the current release), converted to hex for MiniMessage (`<#RRGGBB>`). Tailwind 4 defines its colors in OKLCH, and many of them are more saturated than sRGB can show; these values are clipped to sRGB, which is what Minecraft renders.

Use the hue scales for every color that carries meaning (brand, interactive, states, ranks, teams, rarities). Use one gray scale, or plain neutral grays, for the text.

## Starting points for Minecraft text

| Role | Typical step | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| highlight | a custom tint of the brand (OKLCH lightness about 0.90, chroma about 0.065), between the 100 and 200 steps | the brightest text; 50 and 100 read as plain white in game |
| muted | a gray scale's 400, about `#A09DA3` | quiet body text and keys; lighter grays wash out over bright maps |
| separator | a gray scale's 600, or `#5C5C5C` to `#6A686E` | punctuation that structures without being read |
| brand | a hue's 300 to 500 | saturated enough to read as color over the world |
| interactive | the brand's hue, a step lighter than brand | clickable, related to the brand, deeper than highlight |
| success | lime 500, green 400, or a yellow-green between them | vivid; Tailwind green leans blue, and 200 and 300 read as pastel mint |
| warning | orange or amber 400 to 500 | vivid; avoid pale peach |
| error | red or rose 500 to 600 | reds stay readable when darker; 300 reads as pink |
| accents: ranks, teams, rarities, currencies, games, labels, tags | a hue's 400 to 500, six to eight hues | one color per thing, as saturated as the states |

Steps 50 to 200 of a hue are tints, not colors: use them only as a starting point for highlight and rank message tints. Never use 700 to 950 for text outside inventory titles and light signs; Minecraft's dark backgrounds swallow them.

For Bedrock and pre-1.16 players, hex colors are rounded to the 16 vanilla colors. Yellow 400 and 500 round to gold, and lime 400 rounds to gold too, so they can collide with an amber warning there. The palette script checks this exactly; it only matters if those players join.
